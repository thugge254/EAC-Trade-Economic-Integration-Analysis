2. Introduction The desire for regional cooperation and economic integration was among the main factors that led to the establishment of EAC (East Africa Community). The East African Community has its origin dating back to the colonial period. The first EAC was established in 1967, it collapsed in 1977 due to political and economic differences among the partner states (Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania). Around year 1999, the three countries signed a Treaty to establish EAC again and came into force in 2000. Since then, the East African Community (EAC) has expanded both its membership and the scope of regional integration. The Community currently comprises eight Partner States: Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), and Somalia, which became the newest member in 2023. The EAC is headed by the Secretary General (SG), who serves as the principal executive and accounting officer of the Community. The Secretary General is appointed by the Summit of Heads of State for a single, non-renewable term of five years on a rotational basis among the Partner States. The current Secretary General is Ambassador Stephen Patrick Mbundi of the United Republic of Tanzania, who assumed office in April 2026.
